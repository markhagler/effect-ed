Lesson 2

Promises: The Receipt

A Promise is a receipt for a value that isn't ready yet. You order a smoothie, you get a receipt, and the receipt says "your smoothie will be here soon, or we'll tell you why not."

The story

She orders. Sam doesn't hand her a smoothie. Sam hands her a receipt with a number on it.
The receipt is not the smoothie. It's a promise of one. And notice: the blender started the moment she ordered.
Two endings. Her number is called and here's the smoothie. Or: "Sorry, we're out of mango." Either way, the receipt is finished.

Three things can be true about that receipt at any moment:

  • Pending: still blending. You're holding the receipt, waiting.
  • Fulfilled: your number is called, here's your smoothie. The receipt "turned into" the value.
  • Rejected: "Sorry, we're out of mango." The receipt turned into a reason it failed.

Once a receipt is fulfilled or rejected, it never changes again. A smoothie doesn't un-blend.

What you can do with a receipt

You can write instructions on the back of it:

  • .then(...) means "when it's ready, do this with it."
  • .catch(...) means "if it fails, do this instead."
orderSmoothie("mango")
  .then((smoothie) => console.log("Yum:", smoothie))
  .catch((reason) => console.log("Oh no:", reason))

The order machine

Pick a flavor and order. Watch the receipt change state, and watch which instruction on the back of it runs. Kiwi is out of stock today.


    

  


  
async and await: writing it like a normal story

  
Writing everything in .then chains gets awkward. So JavaScript lets you write it top to bottom:


async function lunch() {
  const smoothie = await orderSmoothie("mango")   // wait here, but Sam stays busy elsewhere
  console.log("Yum:", smoothie)
}

  
    
    
  • async on a function means: "this function hands back a receipt, not the answer."
    • 
    
  • await means: "pause this story until the receipt is fulfilled, then continue." The cook is not frozen while we wait. Other tickets still get done. It's only this story that pauses.
    • 
  

  
If the receipt is rejected, await throws, and you catch it with a normal try/catch:


async function lunch() {
  try {
    const smoothie = await orderSmoothie("mango")
    console.log("Yum:", smoothie)
  } catch (reason) {
    console.log("Oh no:", reason)
  }
}


  
Three things a receipt can't tell you

  

  
Check yourself

  
1. Is the receipt the smoothie? What is it, then?No. It's a promise of a smoothie: a placeholder that will turn into either the smoothie or a reason you didn't get one.

  
2. When does the blender start: when you order, or when you read the receipt?When you order. Creating the Promise starts the work. Reading the receipt (calling .then) doesn't start or stop anything.

  
3. What are the three states? Can a receipt go from Fulfilled back to Pending?Pending, fulfilled, rejected. No. Once settled, a Promise never changes again.

  
4. Your friend says "await freezes the whole program." Use the kitchen to explain why they're wrong.await pauses one story (one function) until its receipt is ready. Sam keeps taking other tickets off the rail meanwhile. The truck never freezes; only that one customer's story is waiting.
